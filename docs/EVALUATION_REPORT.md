# Experiment and Evaluation Report

The hypothesis under test: for rural network conditions, context-aware slicing with RL-based adaptation and
autonomous self-healing delivers better QoS, utilisation, continuity and resilience than static or context-unaware
slicing.

The questions, hypotheses, metrics and statistics are fixed in
[thesis/research_design.md](thesis/research_design.md). Every number here is generated by `experiments/report.py`
into [thesis/results.md](thesis/results.md), which has the full tables. This report summarises it and introduces no
new numbers.

All results come from one reproducible run of `run_pipeline.py --mode full` on the final code:

* manifest: `results/evidence/pipeline_full_manifest.json`;
* every stage ok;
* 665 runs, all made with the same code and model fingerprints.

## 1. Setup

* **Testbed.** A three-tier rural network in Mininet:
  * 2 UEs per service (healthcare, education, agriculture, general);
  * a 100 Mb/s full-duplex rural uplink as the only contention point;
  * edge and core clouds;
  * a 20 ms fiber backhaul with a standby 50 Mb/s backup.

  Enforcement is HTB + fq_codel per direction; switches are Linux bridges in user namespaces.
* **Traffic.** Seeded parametric application models (VBR video, VoIP, IoT, TCP web/bulk/DASH) at context-specific
  design demand, 80–145 Mb/s offered.
* **Real 5G core.** Open5GS + UERANSIM with a 10 Mb/s UPF bottleneck.

## 2. Policies

All policies use the same scenarios, seeds, offered traffic, capacity and failures, paired by (condition, seed).

| Policy | Role |
|---|---|
| `no_slicing`, `static_equal`, `tgpp` | No slicing; static equal shares; technology-centric 3GPP slices |
| `proportional`, `context_unaware` | Context weights without demand awareness; demand-aware without context |
| `proposed` | The audited context-aware demand-aware heuristic (weighted split, borrowing) |
| `rl_cem`, `rl_ppo`, `rl_online` | Learned priority multipliers (simulator-trained; online CEM) |
| `qos_aware` (B) | Per-direction allocation for SLA targets: demands first, explicit degradation order, anti-starvation guard, loss-driven burst headroom, SLA-derived CoDel profiles |
| `qos_adaptive` (B+C) | + reactive re-planning within 1 s; demand-ramp, queue-growth and clock-context prediction |
| `rl_residual` (B+C+D) | + PPO residual rate corrections (trained on the calibrated direction-aware simulator) |
| `rl_residual_online` | + online CEM on residual actions from verified live transitions |
| Healing variants | full / reactive_only / no_healing |
| Classification | host (address rules) / slicenet (mixed UE) / slicenet_all (every flow) |

## 3. Metrics

* **Utility and satisfaction:** weighted utility (priority-weighted demand satisfaction); per-slice demand
  satisfaction.
* **Per-slice QoS:** p95/p99 one-way delay from packet-matched captures; loss; jitter.
* **SLA-met share:** share of 5 s flow windows meeting the application's SLA.
* **Class SLA summaries:** mean and worst class SLA share over the four slices; transient SLA in the 10 s after a
  context change.
* **Resilience:** MTTD and MTTR; outage (longest delivery gap of core-bound flows); packets lost; verified restore
  time.
* **Control:** allocation changes applied and kernel-verified; RL transitions.

## 4. Data provenance and splits

* **Synthetic (path A).** `python -m dataset.rsnsd --seed 42`: 108 000 records, 18 scenarios, contract
  `rsnsd-contract-2.0`. Byte-reproducible (sha256 `6421db4c…`); RSNSD statistical validation 90.77/100.
* **Measured (path B).** 18 collection runs, 4 662 packet-matched windows; contract-valid, none rejected.
  5G runs are stamped `5g_core_measured`.
* **Classifier splits.** Synthetic classifiers hold out whole RSNSD scenarios; measured classifiers hold out run × UE
  groups, and the CNN validates on groups carved from training.
* **Transfer.** Tested on held-out measured groups only.
* **RL seeds.** Training, validation (2000…) and test (1000…) seeds are disjoint.
* **Contended-regime evaluation.** Each evaluation round used fresh seeds (§6).

## 5. Results

### Supporting the hypothesis

* **Self-healing works (H4a, H4b, H10).**
  * Fiber-cut outage ≤ 0.17 s.
  * Predictive switchover happens before the cut in every run, with less outage than reactive-only (0.011 s vs
    0.160 s).
  * Against no healing, core-bound outage falls by about 10 s (paired p = 0.008).
  * 14 recoveries verified, 0 unverified.
* **QoS-targeted control protects the critical class in contended regimes** (contended-regime study, 40 pairs).
  * Healthcare SLA-met share rises by +0.070 against `proposed` (p = 0.0002, survives Bonferroni).
  * Disaster 0.78 → 0.93; medical emergency 0.67 → 0.90; night medical alert 0.79 → 0.94.
  * On a 50 Mb/s harvest uplink the worst-off class improves from 0.75 to 0.94.
  * Against static slicing the worst-off class is better by +0.054 (Bonferroni-significant).
* **Slicing protects healthcare over no slicing.** Healthcare SLA-met share is 0.892 vs 0.780 (p = 0.0007,
  Bonferroni), an exploratory finding.
* **Service identification (H5a, H14, H6).**
  * Measured macro F1 is 0.953 (RF), 0.885 (CNN), and 0.946 on held-out synthetic scenarios.
  * SliceNet classifying **every** flow (no address rules) is non-inferior to address rules on per-class SLA
    (Δ 0.002, p = 0.68). It decides in a median ≈ 1.5 s, with 89 % accuracy at first decision and 92 % after
    confirmed reclassification.
  * Synthetic-only models do not transfer (zero-shot F1 0.10; 0.91 with 50 % measured groups).
* **Design elements matter (H2).** Removing surplus redistribution or work conservation lowers utility.
* **Real 5G core (H8).** Disaster healthcare p95 is 349 vs 798 ms without slicing. The school difference is within
  noise.

### Contradicting or qualifying the hypothesis

* **The healthcare gain is paid for by lower-priority slices (H11 not supported on its stated metric).**
  * Agriculture shares the uplink and falls from 1.00 to 0.63–0.84 in the emergencies.
  * General gives up a little at 50 Mb/s.
  * On the pre-stated unweighted mean over the four classes, `qos_adaptive` is 0.016 below `proposed` (p = 0.005).
    Weighted utility is unchanged.
  * This follows the declared context priority (healthcare 10–15 : agriculture 1) and was not tuned away.
* **Context awareness per se (H9 not supported).** `proposed` vs `context_unaware` differs by +0.0005 utility
  (p = 0.58).
* **Prediction (H13 not supported).** Reactive 1 s re-planning, ramp, queue and clock-context prediction fired 144
  times, but `qos_adaptive` vs `qos_aware` differs by −0.001 (p = 0.68).
* **RL (H12 not supported; H3 marginal).**
  * Residual PPO gains +0.3–0.5 % in simulation (2 of 3 seeds significant) but equals its heuristic on the network
    (Δ 0.002, p = 0.20).
  * The earlier priority-multiplier RL gains 3–4 % in simulation, and on the network only a marginal PPO edge in the
    final run (Δ 0.0033 utility, p = 0.011), which is not Bonferroni-significant and was not reproduced in three
    earlier full runs.
  * Online residual RL ran 267 verified live transitions without a single enforcement failure, far too few to show
    learning.
* **Slicing vs no slicing on total utility (H1a not supported).** The benefit is redistribution toward critical
  services.
* **Robustness (H7 not supported).** Proposed ≥ static in 9 of 12 capacity/load settings.
* **Core-switch failure.** Diagnosed and escalated, not healable.

### Where the approach provides no meaningful improvement

* Nominal capacity with every demand satisfiable (normal, school and harvest at 100 Mb/s).
* Downlink overload by elastic TCP (school at 50 Mb/s or 1.5×), where the weighted heuristic does as well.
* Severe uplink overload (disaster at 1.5×), where no allocation can meet every class.

## 6. How the contended-regime evaluation was kept honest

Each round used fresh seeds. Only behaviour that violated a stated design requirement was fixed, each fix with a
regression test, and all rounds are reported (`results/evidence/qos_eval_v1/`–`v3/`):

| Round | Seeds | Defect exposed | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 0–4 | Strict priority starved best-effort TCP (general SLA 0.0); burst headroom ignored under overload | Anti-starvation guard (20 % of demand); headroom applied under overload |
| v2 | 5–9 | A starved TCP slice's measured demand, and with it the guard, collapsed (general SLA 0.05) | Guard anchored on the context's design demand for elastic slices |
| v3 | 10–14 | Residual RL could override the guard (general SLA 0.04 under `rl_residual`) | Allocator's protected minimum bounds the residual, live and in the simulator |
| v4 | 15–19 | — (confirmatory; reported above) | — |

Agriculture's degradation in emergencies was judged a declared-priority trade-off and left unchanged.

## 7. Reproducibility

* `run_pipeline.py --mode full` regenerates everything (≈ 6.5 h):
  * datasets, SliceNet and RL models;
  * simulator calibration and residual training;
  * integration tests;
  * 665 runs, tables and the report.
* Runs carry code and model fingerprints, and stale runs are excluded from every table.
* Raw captures are deleted after extraction, so a full run needs ≈ 1 GB of disk, not ≈ 80 GB.
* RL training is deterministic.

## 8. Limitations

* Emulation, not radio; parametric rather than trace-driven traffic.
* Linux bridges in sudo-free runs.
* Emulated OAM.
* The direction-aware simulator's per-run loss RMSE is ~2 pp.
* Online RL is sample-poor.
* The UERANSIM GTP path limits 5G rates.
* The verdicts on H11–H14 are post-diagnosis hypotheses, not pre-registered.
